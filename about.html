    Lived experience. Structured action.

    We bridge authentic disabled-user testing with clear, ticket-ready engineering roadmaps.

    Founded on the principle that compliance alone does not guarantee usability, Source Accessibility connects technical WCAG 2.2 AA standards with real human interactions. We manage Skyscanner's Disabled Tester Panel (DTP), helping high-traffic digital services surface critical blockers and translate research into measurable product improvements.

    The experience behind the approach

    Marie Taylor Founder

    I founded Source Accessibility after an unusual career journey spanning sound engineering, technical quality assurance and archiving in broadcast television and radio, including the BBC and Channel 4. Alongside that, I ran a bespoke tailoring studio because I love clothes, craftsmanship and detail.

    Naturally, my next career move was software development. It seemed to tick the boxes

    My pivot into accessibility arose not from my training, but from a 'dev' job interview where I was presented with an accessibility question and drew a total blank. I recognised a serious gap in my formal training, and I couldn’t believe the scale of it.

    More importantly, I had found something I wanted to understand properly. It was immediately clear that accessibility brought together so much of what I enjoy: technology, problem-solving, research, people and the opportunity to make things genuinely better. And, perhaps unsurprisingly, I found myself rather well suited to it. No tailoring pun intended.

    I sought out a mentor, developed my expertise in quality engineering and UX research, and immersed myself in accessibility. I care deeply about this work. Seeing the impact that good accessibility can have, and having the opportunity to help others create better experiences, is a huge driving force for me.

    Source Accessibility is the culmination of those different strands of experience. The more I learn about accessibility, technology and user experience, the more opportunities I see to make digital experiences work better, and to communicate what needs to change in ways that help people act.

    Ultimately, I care about doing things well. I have seen first-hand how disabling and limiting poor digital experiences can be, and how much they can restrict people’s independence and digital freedom. I also see how much better those experiences can be with the right direction.

    That is how I see my role: not simply finding problems, but being in your corner and helping you work out what better looks like, how to get there, and how to make it stick.

    And I feel very lucky to have met some stellar people along the way, and to have incredible partners and disabled testers in my corner.

    I’m in your corner too.

    Four threads that run through our work

    However you work with Source Accessibility, these four core principles unite lived experience with practical delivery:

    1. Authentic Lived Research

    Task-based studies with disabled and neurodivergent participants using real screen readers, Voice Control, magnification, and switch devices—not simulated personas or generic checklists.

    2. Structured & Ticket-Ready Outputs

    We turn research findings directly into actionable, developer-ready tickets, video clips, and prioritization roadmaps that fit neatly into your existing sprint cycles.

    3. Remediation & Validation

    We don't just find problems and walk away. We work alongside your design and engineering squads to validate fixes, re-test user flows, and ensure solutions hold up in production.

    4. Ongoing Capability Partnership

    Flexible, long-term support through live testing observations, workshops, and mentoring that build lasting accessibility knowledge inside your organization.

    Impact across complex digital products

    Our evidence-led approach supports leading travel platforms, charities, and educational institutions:

    • Skyscanner: Managing the Disabled Tester Panel (DTP) to evaluate core flight, hotel, and booking provider flows across web and mobile platforms.
    • Trefoil Trust: Conducting independent accessibility audits to remove grant application barriers for parents of disabled children.
    • University of St Andrews: Facilitating lived-experience sessions and bringing accessible development principles into the Computer Science curriculum.
