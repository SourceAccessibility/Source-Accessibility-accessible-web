About Source Accessibility

We exist to help organisations design digital experiences that are genuinely usable by disabled and neurodivergent people, not just technically compliant on paper.

Accessibility is not a bolt-on. It is a design and product quality issue that affects research, content, user journeys, technology choices, and organisational culture.

Our mission

Source Accessibility partners with digital teams to uncover barriers that are often invisible in standard QA loops. We bring lived experience, real-world assistive technology testing, and practical guidance to help teams build products that work for everyone.

We believe that accessible design improves outcomes for all users. Clear interfaces, robust content, and thoughtful interaction patterns make services more usable, more resilient, and easier to understand.

How we work

Lived experience first

We centre disabled and neurodivergent perspectives in discovery, research, and testing so product decisions are grounded in actual user needs.

Evidence-based guidance

Our work aligns to WCAG 2.2 AA and the European Accessibility Act, while translating standards into clear product actions and priorities.

Practical, collaborative delivery

We partner with design, engineering, and content teams to build feasible, scalable changes that improve inclusion without slowing delivery.

Why this matters

Many barriers remain invisible until users encounter them in context: a keyboard trap, dense wording, a form that makes no sense with screen reader output, or a service that assumes a mouse or precise motor control. We help teams identify those risks early and fix them before they reach users.

Great accessibility work is part strategy, part design, and part operational discipline. It demands listening, testing, and iteration at every stage of the product lifecycle.
