Learn Directly from Lived Experience

Source Accessibility can facilitate live demonstrations with members of its Disabled Test Panel (DTP), bringing genuine lived experience into product discussions. Participants use their everyday assistive technologies and interaction methods to explore agreed features or digital journeys.

Sessions can reveal unexpected interaction barriers, different navigation strategies, workarounds, additional effort, cognitive friction, barriers affecting independence and opportunities to improve usability. We can also include facilitated Q&A, enabling product, design and engineering teams to ask testers directly about their experiences, approaches and the barriers encountered. Where agreed, follow-up communication channels may continue after the demonstration, arranged with participants’ consent, clear boundaries and appropriate facilitation.

See How Your Product Works in Practice

Demonstrations can use a client’s own website, mobile application, internal platform, prototype, customer journey or third-party product experience. We can explore how interactions behave with JAWS, NVDA and VoiceOver screen readers, Apple Voice Control and Windows Voice Access, keyboard-only navigation, screen magnification, iPhone and Android accessibility features, and other relevant assistive technologies.

Sessions can be tailored to specific features, journeys, user needs or internal learning objectives, making it easier for teams to connect practical accessibility principles to the actual decisions they make every day.

Evidence Your Teams Can See and Share

Short, focused recordings can support accessibility investigation, remediation and internal communication. Depending on scope, recordings may demonstrate a barrier, capture screen reader announcements, show focus and keyboard interaction, illustrate Voice Control difficulties, document navigation or interaction workarounds, show user impact and compare interaction patterns before and after a fix.

Video evidence can complement accessibility audit reports, Jira and developer-ready tickets, inclusive research findings, training materials, product and design discussions and stakeholder presentations. It does not replace accessible written descriptions, technical reproduction steps or clear recommendations. Where recordings feature participants, their consent and agreed recording permissions must be respected.

From Observation to Understanding

Demonstrations create opportunities for practical learning and discussion. Depending on scope, sessions may include facilitated observation, live Q&A with disabled testers, product-specific accessibility discussions, technical questions and demonstrations, follow-up workshops, knowledge-sharing channels and recorded examples for internal learning.

The aim is not simply to show what failed, but to help teams understand how the barrier affects real people and what can be improved. Where agreed, Source Accessibility can also support follow-up channels through the client’s existing communication tools, such as Slack or other approved platforms, with any continued participant involvement arranged and scoped appropriately.
