WCAG 2.2 AA, EAA and Accessibility Requirements

Technical evaluations can be scoped against WCAG 2.2 Level AA and, where relevant, EN 301 549 and technical requirements connected with the European Accessibility Act (EAA). UK engagements may also consider the Equality Act 2010 and Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) Accessibility Regulations 2018.

EAA obligations depend on the products, services and jurisdictions involved. A WCAG audit provides technical evidence but does not establish EAA compliance or replace legal advice.

Beyond Automated Accessibility Scanning

Automated checks can identify some common issues, but cannot find every barrier or explain its impact. We combine them where useful with expert-led manual WCAG accessibility testing and assistive technology evaluation.

Depending on scope, we examine keyboard access, screen reader compatibility, names, roles and states, focus, forms and errors, dynamic content, Voice Control, magnification, reflow, mobile and alternative input. For focused compatibility testing, see Assistive Technology Health Checks.

Research with disabled and neurodivergent participants can add lived-experience insight when separately included in scope; it is not part of every audit. See Inclusive Research & Disabled-User Testing.

Findings Your Teams Can Act On

Structured findings explain what is happening, where it occurs, which requirements may be affected, its potential impact and how to reproduce and address it.

Issue and Technical Context

Descriptions of affected components and accessibility failures, with relevant browsers, operating systems, assistive technologies and interaction methods.

Reproduction and User Impact

Steps for developers and QA teams to recreate an issue, alongside an explanation of practical effects on people using different interaction methods.

Standards Mapping

Relevant WCAG success criteria and other applicable technical requirements, identified in the context of the agreed audit scope.

Remediation and Evidence

Practical recommendations for design and engineering teams, supported by screenshots, video clips or assistive technology demonstrations where appropriate.

Findings can be formatted as developer-ready recommendations or Jira-compatible tickets to fit established engineering workflows.

Prioritise. Remediate. Validate.

Prioritised roadmaps can consider user impact, critical journey dependencies, recurring component and design-system issues, reusable fixes, sequencing and retesting. They help teams focus resources and address systemic causes rather than repeatedly fixing symptoms.

Outputs can inform sprint planning, backlogs, product roadmaps, QA and accessibility governance. Your organisation remains responsible for implementation and delivery decisions.

Expert-Led Testing. AI-Assisted Efficiency.

Where appropriate, AI-assisted workflows can organise observations, map evidence to agreed assertions, identify possible patterns and prepare structured reports. Accessibility specialists lead the evaluation and verify outputs; AI does not replace manual testing, expert judgement or independently determine WCAG conformance. Client information is not submitted to external AI services without appropriate agreement.
