Measure progress, not just compliance. Establish meaningful accessibility baselines across digital products and user journeys with structured, repeatable testing.

Structured Testing. Comparable Evidence.

We translate complex digital journeys into structured scenarios and granular, testable assertions. Consistent criteria let teams compare outcomes across product versions, platforms, assistive technologies and testing cycles.

A measurement framework can record pass, fail and not-tested outcomes; task completion and independence; test environments; user impact; practical barriers; and supporting observations. Quantitative measures can support comparison, with context and user impact alongside results—not a single score that treats every barrier as equal.

Where useful, prioritisation or weighting can reflect agreed objectives and impact. This gives product teams and decision-makers transparent evidence without obscuring the detail behind a metric.

Numbers Show Change. People Explain Why.

Quantitative accessibility metrics can reveal patterns, but do not always show how barriers affect the people encountering them. Where included in scope, moderated research with disabled and neurodivergent participants can explain workarounds and additional effort, challenge assumptions about task success and reveal barriers expert testing may not identify.

Expert-led manual accessibility testing and participant research are complementary. Lived-experience findings add context to measured outcomes and help teams assess whether changes improve real-world usability.

From Baseline to Better Decisions

Repeatable accessibility progress tracking can compare releases, highlight recurring barriers, detect regressions, track remediation and reveal systemic component issues across key journeys. Findings can help teams decide where further accessibility investment is most useful.

Benchmarking is intended to support action, not simply produce performance statistics. Depending on your needs, evidence can inform improvement roadmaps, QA processes, reporting and accessibility maturity discussions, as well as ongoing product decisions.

Clear Measures. Actionable Insight.

Depending on scope, clients may receive an accessibility measurement framework, baseline assessment, structured scenarios and assertions, outcome summaries, comparative findings, assistive technology evidence, participant research findings, prioritised barriers and progress reports.

Recommendations, regression findings and validation results help product teams, accessibility leads and decision-makers understand progress and choose improvements. Dashboards or scorecards can be commissioned where appropriate; they are not assumed as a standard output.
